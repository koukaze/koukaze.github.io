CAMBRIDGE, UK
FCC-ee / MPhil research project

Transformer-based
jet-flavour tagging

[R.01]

University of Cambridge · Supervisor: Dr Matthew Monk

How can physical information help distinguish jets with similar signatures? I built a ROOT-to-PyTorch pipeline for 1.2 million simulated jets and adapted a six-flavour Transformer baseline. I then added pairwise attention bias to a light-flavour specialist, using ablation studies to examine the contribution of charge-pair information.

TransformersParticle representationsFeature ablation
Read project details

I combined the specialist with the baseline through probability-level soft fusion, preserving the baseline’s total light-flavour probability while redistributing it among strange, up and down jets.

The work included GPU training with Slurm, hyperparameter comparisons and evaluation across jet kinematics. It strengthened my interest in understanding which physical information a model uses and how its architecture affects what it learns.

PandaX / Research internship

UV spectroscopy for
trace krypton detection

[R.02]

Tsung-Dao Lee Institute, Shanghai Jiao Tong University
Supervisors: Professor Jianglai Liu and Dr Jiaqi Hui

Trace krypton is a source of background in xenon dark matter detectors. I contributed to experimental design and apparatus selection for UV spectroscopy, investigated krypton and xenon excimer emission, and assessed KrXe emission as a potential signature. I also acquired digitiser data and implemented a random forest classifier to filter oscillatory background noise.

UV spectroscopyData acquisitionRandom forests
Read project details

My contributions included apparatus procurement, Linux-based digitiser control and analysis of the measured signals. The project connected hands-on measurement with the practical problem of separating useful signals from instrumental background.

LONDON, UK
LUX-ZEPLIN / MSci research project

Identifying gas-phase
backgrounds in LZ

[R.03]

University College London
Supervisors: Dr Aiham Al Musalhi and Dr Amy Cottle

Gas-phase interactions can overlap the signals of interest in a liquid-xenon detector. I developed an unsupervised Gaussian mixture model to identify these backgrounds without reliable event-by-event gas labels. I used boosted decision trees to investigate discriminating features and refined the clustering with physics-informed weighting.

Unsupervised learningGaussian mixturesSignal acceptance
Read project details

Initial clusters depended too strongly on reconstructed position. I down-weighted position inputs using reconstruction quality and studied the effect of giving greater weight to the S2 top–bottom asymmetry.

I evaluated the selection using calibration-event acceptance and its effect on gas-like backgrounds in the WIMP region of interest, connecting classification performance to an experimental objective.

Cosmology+

Particle physics: experiment and theory

High energy physics+
MPHIL COURSEWORK · A3 HIGH ENERGY PHYSICS

Experimental. I analysed supplied Bₛ → K⁺π⁻K⁻π⁺ samples using particle-identification cuts, alternative mass hypotheses and a boosted decision tree, followed by an extended mass fit and sWeighted invariant-mass projections.
